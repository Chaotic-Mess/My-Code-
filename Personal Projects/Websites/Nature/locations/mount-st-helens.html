Overview

Mount St. Helens in Washington State is renowned for its catastrophic 1980 eruption—the most economically destructive volcanic event in U.S. history. A lateral blast removed the summit, reducing the volcano's height and devastating surrounding forests.

The post‑eruption landscape now serves as a natural laboratory for ecological succession and volcanic recovery. A growing lava dome within the horseshoe‑shaped crater and persistent glacier formation highlight dynamic ongoing processes.

Key Facts

💨 Lateral Blast

Side explosion flattened 600 km² of forest in minutes.

📉 Height Loss

Summit dropped from 2,950 m to 2,549 m after collapse.

🌱 Ecological Renewal

Rapid recolonization by hardy species informs restoration science.

🧊 Crater Glacier

Fast‑growing glacier encircles the lava dome—rare in an active crater.

🔬 Dome Building

2004–2008 eruptions added spines and new lava to inner crater.

🛰️ Monitoring

Dense seismic and GPS networks track magmatic movement.
