Overview

Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa and the tallest free‑standing mountain in the world, rising abruptly from the plains of Tanzania. It is a dormant volcanic complex composed of three cones: Kibo (the summit), Mawenzi and Shira.

Its shrinking glaciers crown a mosaic of ecological zones from cultivated foothills to rainforest, moorland, alpine desert and the arctic summit. Popular trekking routes allow non‑technical climbers to experience dramatic altitude transitions and unique biodiversity.

Key Facts

🧊 Vanishing Ice

Glaciers have lost over 80% of their area since 1912; some may disappear within decades.

🌿 Ecological Zones

Five distinct vegetation zones present a living altitude laboratory.

🎒 Accessible Summit

Requires no technical climbing—primary challenge is acclimatization to altitude.

🌋 Volcanic Cones

Three cones: Kibo (dormant), Mawenzi (eroded), Shira (collapsed). Summit crater still hosts fumaroles.

🌌 Night Ascents

Final push often begins at midnight for sunrise over the African plains.

🩺 Altitude Risks

Rapid gain makes AMS, HAPE and HACE key hazards without proper pacing.
