Overview

Cotopaxi is one of Ecuador’s most iconic volcanoes, a near‑perfect conical stratovolcano rising above paramo grasslands. Its summit glacier crowns frequent fumaroles, while historical eruptions shaped regional drainage and lahar risk zones.

Situated along the Pacific Ring of Fire, Cotopaxi is part of the Ecuadorian volcanic arc generated by subduction of the Nazca Plate. Mountaineers are drawn to its accessible high altitude and dramatic dawn silhouettes.

Key Facts

🧊 Summit Glacier

High tropical ice offers climate change indicators.

🌋 Lahar Potential

Ice melt + ash can mobilize destructive mudflows.

🧭 Climber Magnet

Popular acclimatization objective for Andean expeditions.

🌬️ Fumarolic Activity

Persistent gas vents signal shallow heat sources.

🛰️ Monitored Unrest

Recent activity (2015–16) elevated alert levels.

📐 Symmetry

Classic cone shape used in volcanic morphology studies.
