Overview

Mount Everest, known as Sagarmatha in Nepali and Chomolungma in Tibetan, stands at 8,849 meters (29,032 feet) above sea level, making it Earth's highest mountain. Located in the Mahalangur Himal sub-range of the Himalayas on the border between Nepal and Tibet.

First successfully summited by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay in 1953, Everest has since become the ultimate mountaineering challenge. The mountain attracts hundreds of climbers each year, though its extreme altitude, harsh weather conditions, and treacherous terrain make it one of the most dangerous peaks to climb.

Key Facts

🏔️ Death Zone

Above 8,000 meters is the "Death Zone" where oxygen levels are insufficient to sustain human life for extended periods. Climbers require supplemental oxygen.

🧗 First Ascent

Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay, a Sherpa of Nepal, were the first confirmed climbers to reach the summit on May 29, 1953.

🌡️ Extreme Weather

Summit temperatures can plunge to -60°C (-76°F) in winter, with wind speeds exceeding 200 mph creating life-threatening wind chill.

⏱️ Climbing Season

Most summit attempts occur in May and September when weather conditions are most favorable, during brief "weather windows."

📏 Growing Taller

Everest continues to grow approximately 4mm per year due to tectonic plate movement, pushing the Himalayas higher.

🎒 Base Camps

Two main base camps exist: South Base Camp in Nepal at 5,364m and North Base Camp in Tibet at 5,150m altitude.
