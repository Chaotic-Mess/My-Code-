Overview

Mount Fuji is Japan's tallest peak and an active stratovolcano that last erupted in 1707. Rising to 3,776 meters above sea level, this iconic symmetrical cone is one of the world's most recognizable mountains.

Revered as a sacred mountain in Japanese culture, Mount Fuji has been a source of artistic inspiration for centuries. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Japan's "Three Holy Mountains," attracting millions of climbers and pilgrims each year during the official climbing season from July to September.

Key Facts

🎨 Cultural Icon

Featured in countless works of Japanese art, most famously in Hokusai's "The Great Wave off Kanagawa" and his series "Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji."

⛰️ Climbing Destination

Over 300,000 people climb Mount Fuji annually. The official climbing season runs from early July to mid-September when mountain huts are open.

🌸 UNESCO Heritage

Designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013 as "Fujisan, sacred place and source of artistic inspiration."

🔥 Last Eruption

The most recent eruption occurred in 1707 during the Edo period, lasting for 16 days and covering Edo (Tokyo) in volcanic ash.

🏔️ Five Lakes

The Fuji Five Lakes region at the mountain's northern base offers stunning views and is a popular tourist destination year-round.

⛩️ Spiritual Significance

Considered sacred in Shinto religion, with shrines at the summit and base. Historically, women were forbidden from climbing until 1868.
