Overview

Krakatoa (Krakatau) is a volcanic island group in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra. In 1883 its cataclysmic eruption generated colossal explosions heard thousands of kilometers away, triggered tsunamis and caused global atmospheric effects.

The modern active vent, Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa"), emerged from the submerged caldera in 1927 and continues to build itself through frequent strombolian eruptions and lava flows.

Key Facts

💥 1883 Blast

One of the loudest sounds in recorded history; pressure waves circled the globe.

🌊 Tsunami Impact

Generated deadly waves killing over 30,000 people along nearby coasts.

🌎 Climate Effects

Injected aerosols causing vivid sunsets and global temperature anomalies.

🌱 Anak Growth

New cone rises roughly 5–10 m per year during active phases.

🛰️ Collapse 2018

Flank failure triggered deadly tsunami highlighting ongoing hazards.

🔬 Monitoring

Indonesian volcanology center maintains continuous surveillance.
