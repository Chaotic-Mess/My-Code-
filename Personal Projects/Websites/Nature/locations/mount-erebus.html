Overview

Mount Erebus rises from Ross Island in Antarctica, hosting one of the world's few persistent convecting lava lakes. Its stark juxtaposition of ice and molten rock offers unparalleled insights into magmatic stability under polar conditions.

Named by Sir James Clark Ross after the ship HMS Erebus, the volcano produces distinctive anorthoclase phenocrysts in its alkaline lava. Ice towers around fumaroles and wind‑sculpted snow create surreal geothermal microhabitats.

Key Facts

🌋 Lava Lake

Persistent lava lake allows long‑term gas & thermal studies.

❄️ Ice Towers

Fumarolic vents build hollow ice chimneys from freezing vapor.

🧪 Unique Chemistry

Alkaline phonolite lava hosts large anorthoclase crystals.

📡 Remote Monitoring

Autonomous instruments endure extreme cold and isolation.

🚁 Difficult Access

Field campaigns rely on limited seasonal flights & support.

🌐 Scientific Window

Model system for high‑latitude volcanic degassing.
