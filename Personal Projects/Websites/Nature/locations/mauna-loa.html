Mauna Loa - Earth's Largest Volcano

Overview

Mauna Loa, meaning "Long Mountain" in Hawaiian, is the world's largest active volcano by volume and area. Rising 4,169 meters above sea level, this massive shield volcano dominates the island of Hawaii and has shaped the landscape through countless eruptions over millennia.

Nicknamed "Hell's Gate" by early explorers who witnessed its ferocious eruptions, Mauna Loa has erupted 33 times since 1843. Its lava flows have reached the ocean on both sides of the Big Island, creating new land and threatening communities. The volcano's summit caldera, Moku'āweoweo, is one of the largest volcanic craters in the world.

Key Facts

🌊 Largest by Volume

Mauna Loa is the largest volcano on Earth by volume, containing approximately 75,000 cubic kilometers of rock - more than all other Hawaiian volcanoes combined.

🔥 Recent Activity

Most recent eruption was in November 2022, the first since 1984. Lava flows threatened nearby communities but stopped before reaching populated areas.

⚡ Fast Flows

Mauna Loa's gentle slopes allow lava to flow rapidly - some historic flows traveled over 50 km in just hours, reaching speeds of 35 km/h.

🌡️ Atmospheric Research

The Mauna Loa Observatory, established in 1956, conducts crucial atmospheric research including the famous Keeling Curve measuring CO2 levels.

🏔️ Submarine Giant

When measured from its base on the ocean floor, Mauna Loa rises over 9,000 meters - taller than Mount Everest from sea level.

🌺 Cultural Heritage

Sacred to Native Hawaiians, Mauna Loa is home to Pele, the volcano goddess. The summit is considered a spiritual place requiring respect.
