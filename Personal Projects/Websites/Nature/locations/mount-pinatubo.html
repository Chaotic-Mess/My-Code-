Overview

Mount Pinatubo in the Philippines produced one of the largest eruptions of the 20th century in June 1991. Massive Plinian columns injected sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, temporarily cooling global temperatures and altering atmospheric chemistry.

Exemplary hazard forecasting combined seismic, gas, and ground deformation data—saving thousands of lives through timely evacuations. Post‑eruption lahars reshaped river systems for years, while a crater lake gradually formed in the caldera.

Key Facts

🌡️ Climate Impact

SO₂ aerosols cooled Earth ~0.5°C temporarily.

🚨 Forecast Success

Joint PHIVOLCS & USGS effort set preparedness benchmark.

🌊 Crater Lake

Hydrothermal lake now fills the collapse basin.

🌋 Plinian Columns

Eruption produced towering multi‑phase ash plumes.

🌧️ Lahar Chains

Rainfall remobilized ash into long‑lived mudflows.

🛰️ Satellite Legacy

Advanced aerosol tracking transformed climate modeling.
